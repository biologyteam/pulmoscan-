AI-based asthma detection through breath analysis

A research prototype that records breathing sounds with a stethoscope and reads gas, temperature and humidity sensors from an electronic nose, to investigate whether breath analysis can support asthma detection with machine learning.

About the project

Asthma changes how a person breathes, and may change the chemistry of the air they exhale. Breath can be sampled without any invasive procedure, which makes it an attractive target for low-cost screening research.

The central objective is AI-based asthma detection using breath analysis. The physical prototype has two independent components: respiratory audio analysis (a stethoscope recording checked for wheeze-like and crackle-like sounds) and an electronic nose (gas sensors, temperature and humidity read by an ESP32). Each is processed and reported separately. Finding a wheeze or crackle does not confirm asthma, and no asthma classification is shown unless a model has passed validation.

How it works

  1. Data collectionBreathing sounds from a stethoscope connected to the laptop, and live gas, temperature and humidity readings from the electronic nose.
  2. Data processingAudio is resampled and turned into acoustic features. Sensor readings are checked before they are shown. Each data type has its own pipeline.
  3. Machine learningSeparate wheeze and crackle models analyze the audio. There is no gas-analysis model yet.
  4. ResultsModel scores with their thresholds, saved sessions, history over time and printable reports.
