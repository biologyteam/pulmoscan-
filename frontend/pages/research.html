Research & results

AI-based asthma detection using breath analysis: problem, methods, data, validation and limits.

Research problem

Asthma is a chronic respiratory condition. Diagnosis can be slow and often needs specialist equipment, which is hard to find in some regions. Our central question is whether breath analysis, interpreted by machine learning, can support asthma detection. This prototype studies two signals separately: the sound of breathing and the gas composition of breath.

Background

Breathing sounds such as wheezes and crackles carry information about the airways. Volatile organic compounds in exhaled breath are studied as chemical markers of inflammation, and electronic noses use arrays of gas sensors to record such patterns.

Methodology
  • Respiratory audio analysis: a stethoscope recording is captured on the laptop, then analyzed by one model for wheeze-like sounds and one for crackle-like sounds. Reported separately, never as an asthma probability.
  • Electronic nose: MQ-135, MQ-7 and MQ-2 gas sensors with temperature and humidity, read by an ESP32 and shown live. A gas-analysis model is future work.
  • One independent pipeline per signal.
Dataset description
  • ICBHI Respiratory Sound Database: annotated breathing recordings, used to train the wheeze and crackle models.
  • Our own electronic-nose dataset (planned): labelled readings from our hardware, to be collected at a hospital. No gas model exists until it is collected.

These sources measure different things and are never treated as interchangeable.

Data preprocessing
  • Audio: mono, resampled to 4 kHz, cut into 3-second windows, 65 acoustic features (Librosa/NumPy).
  • Electronic nose: readings are checked (finite numbers, sensor ranges) before they are shown. Baseline correction and response features are planned for the model.
Machine learning algorithms

Random forests for the wheeze and crackle models, with fixed hyper-parameters and a decision threshold chosen on validation data rather than test data. The electronic-nose algorithm will be chosen on its own dataset.

Evaluation metrics

Sensitivity, specificity, precision, F1-score, ROC-AUC and confusion matrices on a patient-wise held-out test set. See Model Performance.

Limitations

This is a research prototype, not a clinically validated device. The audio models label wheezes and crackles, not asthma, perform only modestly on held-out data, and were trained on a public dataset recorded with other equipment. Recording position, noise and breathing effort change the result. The electronic nose has no analysis model yet, and MQ gas sensors are not diagnostic tools.
