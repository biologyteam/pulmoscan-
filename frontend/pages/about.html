About

Why we are studying breath analysis for asthma, how the system is built, and what is and is not implemented.

The idea

The central objective is AI-based asthma detection using breath analysis. The prototype records breathing sounds with a stethoscope and analyzes them for wheeze-like and crackle-like patterns, and separately reads an electronic nose built from gas sensors, temperature and humidity. Each component is independent and reported honestly. The goal is research, not diagnosing users.

Objectives

  • Investigate AI-based asthma detection through breath analysis.
  • Analyze respiratory sounds (wheeze, crackle) from a stethoscope connected to the laptop.
  • Collect real readings from the electronic nose and show their connection status.
  • Build and validate a separate gas-analysis model on our own labelled dataset.
  • Show each component’s status, never unvalidated predictions.
  • Let users follow their saved results over time.
  • Evaluate the limits and potential of the system.

What works today and what is planned

ImplementedAccounts, saved sessions, history and printable reportsEach user sees only their own records.
ImplementedAudio recording from a selected input device, or upload of an existing fileWaveform and spectrogram preview; the recording is saved with its session. Tested with a simulated input device; it still needs to be verified with the actual digital stethoscope and audio interface.
ImplementedWheeze and crackle modelsSeparate scores and thresholds. Trained on the public ICBHI data; held-out performance is modest.
ImplementedElectronic-nose connection status and live readingsConnected, Disconnected, Receiving Data and Error states; latest values and charts from the readings the ESP32 sends.
PlannedSaving electronic-nose readings to a user’s sessionsReadings are currently held in memory only and are lost when the server restarts.
PlannedGas-analysis modelNeeds our own labelled dataset collected at a hospital, then validation. No gas result is shown until then.
PlannedEvaluating the audio models on our own stethoscope recordingsCurrent figures come from a different recording setup.
